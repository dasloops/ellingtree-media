KEY POINTS
- NOTHING IN THIS BATCH SETTLES IT. No record found calls Ruth Pickett Marvin's daughter or
  heir, or calls any of her children his grandchild.
- Support: "Ruth Philbrick" signs as a witness to a deed of Pickett and Mary Marvin of
  Bloomfield Township, 27 Dec 1830 (item 1a).
- New, and it changes the picture: Ruth was not widowed by Benjamin Philbrick. She sued him for
  divorce in Jackson County, 26 Dec 1833, and the Supreme Court (sitting at Gallipolis)
  dissolved the marriage on 12 Apr 1834, for wilful absence. Her petition says he left her in
  Fairfield County "three years ago last June" and was living at or about Louisville, Ky.; it
  names two living children, Pamelia (or Parmelia), 14, and Sarah Ann, 3; and says she had
  lived in Jackson County the past two years. It names no parent of hers (item 5a).
- Against the grandfather-gift idea, or at least not for it: the guardian's deed of 23 Mar
  1857 calls George W. Rice "minor and orphan son of George Rice late of the County ...
  deceased", and sells only "the interest of the said George W. Rice" in the three tracts. It
  does not say how he came by it. Ruth herself bought that interest for $1,440 (item 2a), then
  she and William Cheeseman sold the Jackson tract to Thomas G. Jones for $1,070 and the
  Gallia tracts for $180 (items 2b, 2c).
- A different George W. Rice, with a wife Cynthia, was alive in Jackson County in Dec 1853
  (item 2d). Don't confuse him with Ruth's husband.
- Pickett Marvin deeded his home farm (part of NW 1/4 sec. 19, T8, R17, 182 acres) to his son
  Timothy twice, about 1837 and in 1840 (item 1 list). No deed from Pickett to Philbrick,
  Rice, Cheeseman or Ruth turned up in the full-text hits.

  What:      Ralph Hatten [Hatton, Hatter in the transcript], "as Guardian of
             George W Rice minor and orphan son of George Rice late of the County and State

             interest of the said George W Rice" in the land below; sale reported to and
             confirmed by the court 27 Mar 1857 [the Gallia copy says 23 Mar]; for $1,440 paid
             by Ruth Cheeseman of Jackson County, the purchaser, conveys to her all George W.
             Rice's interest in:
               - E 1/2 SE 1/4 sec. 24, T7, R17, 77 acres, Jackson County;
               - NW 1/4 NW 1/4 sec. 30, T6, R16, 48.05 [?] acres, Gallia County;
               - about 10 acres on Indian Creek, from the SW corner of sec. 19, T6, R16, east
                 to a small maple on the first small branch, down it to main Indian Creek, up
                 the creek to the quarter line, west, then south to the start.
  Dated:     23 Mar 1857; acknowledged before Geo. M. Adams J.P.; witnesses [?] Geo. M. Adams,
             H. H. Adams; received 16 Dec 1857, recorded 7 Jan 1858
  Notes:     This is the sale the Jackson Standard notice of Feb 1857 announced, and Ruth
             herself was the buyer. The deed recites the court's order but not the petition,
             so it does not say how the boy held the land. "Orphan son of George Rice ...
             deceased" points to an inheritance from his father, not from a grandfather, but
             the deed does not say that either. The petition itself (Probate Court, Jan 1857)
             is still to be found.

court-1834-jackson-philbrick/1.jpg, 2.jpg [item 5a]  NOT YET IN REPO
  Collection: Jackson County, Ohio, Supreme Court record (Full-Text)
  Images:    https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSK7-Y9Q6-B  (image 145)
             https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSK7-Y9QD-N  (image 146)
             of 595, "Jackson. Complete Probate Records 1818-1847, 1847-1851, Court Records
             1818-1847, 1847-1851, 1852-1858"; DGS not recorded
  Where:     book pp. 255-257, April Term 1834
  Case:      Ruth Philbrick v. Benjamin Philbrick, petition for divorce
  What:      Filed 26 Dec 1833. Ruth, "now and for two years last an actual resident of said
             County", married Benjamin Philbrick on 4 May 1818 [so the transcript; the
             marriage record in hand gives 3 May] in Gallia County. He took to drink and
             gambling, left and returned several times over four or five years, persuaded her
             to move "on the canal" in Fairfield County, where she cooked for the canal hands,
             and three years ago last June [June 1830] left her there, and is living at or
             about Louisville, Ky. Two children living: "Familie" [Pamelia] and Sarah Ann,
             aged 14 and 3. He owned a fractional lot with a frame house at Baltimore, west
             side of the canal, Fairfield County, worth $200, which he has made some transfer
             of to one John Pugh. She asks for divorce, alimony and dower. Summons returned
             not found. Heard at Jackson 10 Apr 1834 and reserved; decided at Gallia County,
             12 Apr 1834: marriage dissolved for wilful absence of more than three years;
             Pamelia and Sarah Ann to stay in Ruth's care; Benjamin to pay $200 alimony
             within three months. Certified by Francis Lebler [?], clerk, Gallipolis.
  Notes:     Ruth's parents are not named. Benjamin was not dead in 1834; he was divorced.
             Only two children are named as living in Dec 1833, which leaves no room for a
             Philbrick son born 1820-25 unless he had died. Pamelia's age, 14 in Dec 1833,
             puts her birth about 1819.
