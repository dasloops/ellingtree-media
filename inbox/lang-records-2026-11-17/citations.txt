KEY POINTS
- No record found in this batch names Theresia's mother. The question is still open.
- The 1852 marriage: only the civil index was found (George Habercorn and Teresa Lang, Dubuque
  County, book 1, p. 69, cert. 206). The search-results line dates it 27 Oct 1852, not 4 Oct.
  The register image is padlocked. No filmed registers of SS. Peter and Paul, Sherrill, were
  found in the FamilySearch Catalog, so the church entry (the one likely to name parents) was not
  reached.
- 1850: the household is Allois Long 46, Catherine Long 57, Sarah Long 15, Allois Long 12, all
  born Germany (read from the image; the index gives Catherine as 51 and the head as female).
- 1860: Aloysus Lang 54, farmer, b. Bavaria, still has Catharina Lang 61 (b. Bavaria) in his
  household at Sherrill's Mount P.O., Jefferson Twp. So Catherine was alive in 1860, and was
  born in Bavaria by this census (not Switzerland, as the online tree says).
- The younger "Allois" of 1850 is a son, not a daughter: in 1860 he is Aloysus Lang, 21, male,
  farm laborer, with Elizabeth, 26. He is very likely the Alois Lang who married Elizabeth
  Stuckel/Steckel in 1859 and died in Dubuque on 20 Dec 1911 aged 74 (born 24 Dec 1837).
  His death certificate names his father Alois Lang, born Germany, and gives the mother's
  maiden name as "Don't know".
- 1870: Aloys Lang, 63, b. Bavaria, lives in the household of Peter Fries, Jefferson Twp, with
  no wife listed. So Alois senior outlived Catherine or was living apart from her by 1870.
- If Theresia (b. c. 1836) was Alois and Catherine's daughter, Catherine (b. c. 1793-1799)
  was about 37-43 at her birth: late, but possible. The stepmother question is not settled
  either way by these records.
- The Family Tree's "Catherine Hahn, b. c. 1818" (L8WJ-VTM) has no sources attached at all.
