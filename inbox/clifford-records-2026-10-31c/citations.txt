KEY POINTS
- 2 Mar 1813: "Moses Clifford of Washington in the County of Orange & State of Vermont,
  Yeoman" bought Edward and Mary Dodge's home farm in the northerly part of Londonderry, NH
  (about 80 acres plus a 30-acre lot) for $2000 (item 1). This is the father of Orlando
  (Washington, VT, birth cards, batch 1 items 3-3c) moving to Londonderry, where Philander was
  born in Aug 1813 to Moses and Esther Clifford (batch 2, item 7).
- 4 Feb 1817: "Moses Clifford of Londonderry", yeoman, sold the same two parcels to Moses Copp
  of Plaistow for $1750; "Esther Clifford wife of the said Moses" released her dower and signed
  (item 2). Same day he bought 29 acres in Plaistow, NH, from Copp (item 8, not uploaded).
- Indiana: Moses Clifford "of Rush" County bought 80 acres (W 1/2 NE 1/4, Sec. 11, T15N, R9E) on
  11 Aug 1825, patented 1 Jul 1826, and 80 more (E 1/2 NW 1/4 of the same section) on 29 Aug
  1831, patented 5 Aug 1834 (items 3, 6, 7). FamilySearch's index calls him "Dr Moses
  Clifford": "Dr." is the ledger's Debtor heading (the facing page is headed "Cr."), not a title.
- 1839-1840, Rush County Circuit Court: Moses Clifford v. Philander Clifford, in assumpsit and in
  chancery, dismissed at the April term 1840 (item 9).
- 1840: Moses Clifford alone in his household at Greenfield, Hancock County, Indiana, a man aged
  50-60 (item 4). With the 1810 census (26-45) and the 1830 census (50-60, if that is him), this
  points to a birth about 1780.
- Not found: a Hancock County probate for Moses (see NOT FOUND). Moses's wife's maiden name.
  Any record naming Moses's parents.
- Orange County, Vermont: Israel Clifford "late of Corinth" died before 18 Nov 1822 leaving a will
  (Stephen Eaton, executor; item 5). His widow Judith, sister of Peter Severance, said in 1831
  that Israel enlisted with her brother at Deerfield, NH, in 1775 (item 10). The will itself has
  not been found; nothing found so far connects Moses to this family.

    known her about thirty years.
