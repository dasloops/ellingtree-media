This batch follows batch 1 (inbox/marvin-records-2026-11-01/, already filed into the gallery:
the 1830 Marvin-Fletcher deed witnessed by Ruth Philbrick, the 1833-34 Philbrick divorce, the
1857 guardian's deed and Cheeseman sales, the 1839 Rice-Radcliff deed). It holds the 1850
census pages and the results of the deeper search for a record calling Ruth Pickett Marvin's
daughter or heir. Readings marked [checked on image] were read on the image; the rest come
from FamilySearch's AI transcripts and are marked [?] where doubtful.

KEY POINTS
- STILL NOTHING SETTLES IT. No record found calls Ruth Pickett Marvin's daughter or heir, or
  any of her children his grandchild.
- 1850 census: Ruth was not in Pickett's household. She was "Ruth Rice", 47, in Madison
  Township, Jackson County, in the house of George Rice, 69, farmer, born Virginia, real
  estate $1,500, with George Rice, 11, and Eliza Howell, 12. So her husband George Rice was
  alive in July 1850 and died between then and her remarriage in Feb 1853. He owned land
  himself, which makes it likely that the boy's land in 1857 was his father's, not a gift
  from Pickett.
- In the same census Pickett ("Picket Marvin", 82) has NO real estate entered; Dr. Timothy
  Marvin, three houses on, has $4,600. Pickett had deeded his farm to Timothy in 1840. There
  may have been no land left for heirs to divide, which would explain why no heirs' deed or
  partition turns up.
- Pickett's death date: the Find a Grave index (not checked against the stone) gives
  8 June 1852, not June 1853. Six Brothers Cemetery, Jackson County.
  https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:QV2R-HYKS
- No partition 1851-1856: the index to Common Pleas Journal G has no case under Marvin,
  Cheeseman, Philbrick or Radcliff.
- The Jackson County Probate Court's records for the 1850s are not filmed at all (see NOT
  FOUND, 2). George Rice's estate (1850-53), the 1857 guardian's petition, and any
  administration of Pickett's estate can only be had from the court.
