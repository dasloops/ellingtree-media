FINDINGS - Was Ruth Marvin (1802-1891) a daughter of Pickett Marvin (1768-1852/3)?
FamilySearch searches of 1 Nov 2026. Notes for whoever files this into the Ellingtree
Research Archive. Full citations, arks and transcriptions are in citations.txt in this
folder (batch b) and in batch 1, already filed into the gallery.

==========================================================================================
WHERE IT STANDS

Not proven. No record found online calls Ruth Pickett Marvin's daughter or heir, or calls
any of her children his grandchild.

==========================================================================================
WHAT WAS FOUND

Support for the link
- 1880 census, Northfield City, Rice County, Minnesota (8 June 1880): "Cheesman Ruth", 78,
  widowed, keeping house, born Ohio; father born "Conn", mother born "Va." (both columns
  checked on the image). In the 1850 census Pickett Marvin is born "Ct." and his wife Mary
  "Va.". The pairing matches exactly. It is a census answer from an unknown informant and
  names no one, so it supports the link; it does not prove it. With her: grandson "Tripp
  Burtie", 9, born Minnesota, both his parents born Ohio.
  (batch b: census-1880-northfield-cheesman.jpg)
- Deed of Pickett and Mary Marvin of Bloomfield Township to Robert W. Fletcher, 27 Dec 1830.
  Witnesses: John Stephenson and Ruth Philbrick (checked on the image). Mary signed by her
  mark. (batch 1, filed)

Ruth's own life, corrected
- Ruth was divorced from Benjamin Philbrick, not widowed. She filed in Jackson County on
  26 Dec 1833; the decree came on 12 Apr 1834 (Supreme Court, at Gallipolis), for wilful
  absence. He had left her in Fairfield County, on the canal, in June 1830 and was living at
  or about Louisville, Ky. Two children living: "Pamilia and Sarah Ann, aged Fourteen &
  three years". She had lived in Jackson County "for more than two years". Married "the
  fourth day of May ... 1818" in Gallia County (the marriage record says 3 May). Her parents
  are not named. Only two children are named as living in Dec 1833. (batch 1, filed)
- Her second husband, George Rice, was alive in July 1850. 1850 census, Madison Township,
  Jackson County (31 July 1850), dwelling 289: George Rice, 69, farmer, born Va., real estate
  $1,500; Ruth Rice, 47, born Ohio; George Rice, 11; Eliza Howell, 12. So he died between
  July 1850 and her marriage to William Cheeseman in Feb 1853.
  (batch b: census-1850-madison-rice.jpg)
- Permelia Philbrick married Stephen Ratliff (Radcliff) in Gallia County, 16 Apr 1835.

The 1857 land
- The guardian's deed of 23 Mar 1857: "Ralph Hatton of the County of Jackson ... as
  Guardian of George W Rice minor and orphan son of George Rice late of the County and State
  aforesaid deceased", by order of the Probate Court of 12 Jan 1857; sale confirmed 23 Mar
  1857 (not the 27th, as the AI transcript had it). Ruth Cheeseman bought the boy's interest
  for $1,440 in: E 1/2 SE 1/4 sec. 24, T7, R17, 77 acres, Jackson County; NW 1/4 NW 1/4
  sec. 30, T6, R16, 48.10 acres, Gallia County; and about 10 acres on Indian Creek, sec. 19.
  Two days later she and William Cheeseman sold the Jackson tract to Thomas G. Jones for
  $1,070, and the Gallia tracts for $180. (batch 1, filed)
- Because George Rice was alive in 1850 and owned land, the boy's land most likely came
  from his own father, not from Pickett. The deed's "orphan son of George Rice ...
  deceased" fits that.

Pickett's estate
- Pickett owned no real estate in 1850. 1850 census, Bloomfield Township (18 July 1850),
  dwelling 100: "Picket Marvin", 82, farmer, born Ct., real-estate column blank; Mary
  Marvin, 76, born Va.; Matilda Fletcher, 14. Dwelling 104: Timothy Marvin, 37, physician,
  real estate $4,600; Matilda, 34; Watson, 7; Eberle, 4; Marietta, 6/12.
  (batch b: census-1850-bloomfield-marvin.jpg)
- He had deeded his farm (part of NW 1/4 sec. 19, T8, R17, 182 acres) to Timothy, about
  1837 and again in 1840. If nothing was left, there was no estate to divide, which would
  explain why no heirs' deed or partition turns up.
- The Find a Grave index gives Pickett's death as 8 June 1852, not 1853 (Six Brothers
  Cemetery, Jackson County; not checked against the stone).

Not the same man
- A different George W. Rice, with a wife Cynthia, was alive in Jackson County in Dec 1853.
  Don't confuse him with Ruth's husband.

==========================================================================================
RULED OUT ONLINE

- The Common Pleas Journal G, 1851-1856: its own index, read under M (with both overflow
  pages), R, C, H and P, has no case under Marvin, Cheeseman, Philbrick or Radcliff.
- No deed to or by Timothy Marvin, 1854-1870, comes from an heir.
- Mary Marvin appears in no record after 1851.
- Ruth's 1818 marriage entry (by David C. Sayre J.P.) shows no consent or parent.
- The Jackson County Probate Court's records for the 1850s are not filmed at all. The
  FamilySearch catalog has only wills (1819-1885) and dockets from 1879.

==========================================================================================
WHERE PROOF IS MOST LIKELY NOW

A letter to the Jackson County Probate Court, Jackson, Ohio, asking for:
1. The estate of George Rice of Madison Township, died between July 1850 and Feb 1853. It
   should say how the 77 acres were held.
2. The guardianship of George W. Rice, minor, Ralph Hatton guardian: the order of 12 Jan
   1857 and the sale confirmed 23 Mar 1857.
3. Any administration or will of Pickett Marvin, 1852-53.

Still open online
- The R grantee pages of the Jackson deed indexes, 1816-1877, to see who deeded the 77 acres
  to George Rice.
- Ruth's 1891 death entry in the Rice County, Minnesota, register.
- Burtie Tripp's parents, as a lead to Ruth's grandchildren.
