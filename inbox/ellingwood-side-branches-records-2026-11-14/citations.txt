  Transcription (pages 62-64, the Jason Adams entry only; the body follows FamilySearch's

    [p. 62] At a Surrogates Court held at the Village of Saratoga Springs in and for the County of
    Saratoga on the 14th day of February 1863. Present: Cornelius A. Waldron Surrogate.
    Be it remembered that at a Surrogates Court held on the 14th day of February 1863, Charles
    Adams, James M. Gailor and Lewis M. Adams, the persons named as executors in a certain
    instrument dated the 9th day of September 1862, purporting to be the last Will and Testament
    of Jason Adams deceased, appeared and presented said Will for probate; and at the same time
    made satisfactory proof that said Jason Adams died on or about the 9th day of January 1863,
    being at the time of his death an inhabitant of the County of Saratoga, and that he left him
    surviving the following named persons, to wit: Laura Gilbert, Leonard Adams, Harriet Wooley
    wife of Nelson Wooley, Orinda Wooley wife of Harry [?] Wooley and said petitioners, Charles
    Adams and Lewis M. Adams, all of full age and who are all and the only heirs at law and next
    of kin of said deceased. And all of said heirs and next of kin having appeared and waived by
    an instrument in writing the issuing & service upon them of a citation to attend the proof
    and probate of said last Will and Testament, and consenting to the immediate examination of
    the witnesses thereto, and said Will being produced and shown to the attesting witnesses
    thereto, the proof thereof was taken as follows to wit:
    County of Saratoga, Surrogates Court. In the matter of proving the last Will and Testament
    of Jason Adams deceased. Saratoga County SS: Edward Bevins and Edmund R. Woolley of the town
    of Wilton in the County of Saratoga being first duly sworn in open court, on their oaths do
    depose and say, that they are subscribing witnesses to the last Will and Testament of Jason
    Adams late of the town of Wilton in the County of Saratoga, aforesaid, deceased. And these
    deponents further say, that the said deceased did, in the presence of these deponents,
    subscribe his name at the end of the instrument which is now shown to these deponents, and
    which purports to be the last Will and Testament of the said deceased, and which bears date
    on the 9th day of September in the year one thousand eight hundred and sixty two; that the
    said deceased did at the time of subscribing his name to said instrument as aforesaid declare
    the same to be his last Will and Testament; that these deponents did thereupon subscribe
    their own names at the end of said instrument as attesting witnesses to the execution
    thereof, at the request of the said deceased, and in his presence and in the presence of each
    other. [p. 63] That the said deceased at the time of subscribing his name to said instrument
    as aforesaid, was upwards of twenty one years of age; that he appeared to be of sound mind and
    memory, and was not under restraint to the knowledge or belief of these deponents.
    Edward Bevins, E. R. Woolley. Subscribed and sworn this 14th day of February 1863 before me
    C. A. Waldron Surrogate.
    And it appearing from the proof so taken that said instrument was duly executed, and that
    said testator was at the time of executing the same in all respects competent to devise Real
    Estate and not under restraint. And the said Surrogate having inquired into the facts and
    circumstances, and being satisfied of the genuineness and validity of said Will, it is
    ordered, adjudged and decreed that said instrument be admitted to probate as and to prevail
    as and for the last Will and Testament of said Jason Adams deceased. And it is further
    ordered that said Will be recorded with the proof thereof, which said proof is herein before
    recorded, and said Will is in the words and figures
    "In the name of God, Amen. I Jason Adams being of sound mind and well advanced in years and
    considering the uncertainty of this mortal life, blessed be God for the same do make and
    publish this my last Will and Testament in manner and form following that is to say: First.
    I give to my beloved wife Mary or Polly as she is commonally coled the use of all my property
    both Real and personal during her natural life, and at her death all my real estate shall be
    equally divided between my three sons Leonard Adams, Lewis M. Adams and Charles Adams with
    this proviso. All my debts shall be paid out of my Real Estate by Leonard, Lewis and Charles
    and if my Real Estate shall be appraised worth two thousand dollars over and above paying my
    debts then my three sons Leonard, Lewis and Charles shall pay to my three daughters Laura
    Gilbert, Harriet Woolly and Orrinda Woolley one hundred dollars to each of them say Laura
    Harriet and Orrinda to be paid to them within one year after there shall be a division or
    sale of said real estate, and if the Real Estate shall not be worth two thousand dollars
    after all my debts are paid, then my sons shall pay to my daughters in proportion to the one
    hundred, calling one hundred each out of Two thousand. And I further give and bequeath to my
    three daughters Laura Gilbert, Harriet Woolley and Orrinda Woolley All my household furniture
    comprising Beds and bedding Tables, Crockery Chairs Looking Glasses Stands and in fine all
    that shall be left at the death of my self and my beloved wife which is considered household
    furnity. And I further order that Lewis M shall be paid out of my Real Estate what the barn
    is worth standing near the meeting house deducting what the timber worth standing in the
    woods as it all come of of [p. 64] the land to be divided and owned by me in my life time.
    And I do further order that my personal property that shall be out doors such as cows,
    horses, swine, wagons harness or farming utentials or anything that shall be left out doors
    shall be sold or applide to paying funeral expenses for myself and wife and for head stones
    for me and my wife and if there should not be enough then the remainder shall be taken out of
    the real estate and in case there should be more than to bury us decesent and supply the head
    stones then the surplus shall be put out on interest and given to J. R. Adams son of Charles
    Adams. And I do further order that there shall be twenty five dollars deducted out of any
    just claim that Lewis M. Adams may present against me or the estate for rent due me which he
    had ought to have paid Vischer for rent that was due while he lived and ocupide the land north
    of the road coled the forty two acre lot. In speaking of my out door personal property, it is
    understood to mean all grain I may have on hand, potatoes, hay or fodder of all descriptions.
    There is due from John Han [?] yearly rents of three dollars thirty seven cents, Also from
    Nelson Woolley four dollars and forty four cents which my executors will see that is fixed in
    appraising the land which will reduce the rent of twenty five dollars going to Visscher to
    seventeen dollars and nineteen cents, these rents comes due the first of April in each and
    every year it is my wish the land to be appraised with the seventeen dollars and nineteen
    cents rent as the rents to be collected reduces it to that amount, Sept 9. 1862. Witness my
    hand and seal Jason Adams (L. S.) I do hereby appoint Charles Adams, Lewis M. Adams and
    James M. Gailor [?] as my executors of my estate. Jason Adams.
    The foregoing last will and Testament of Jason Adams containing one sheet of paper ocupide by
    the said testator or will was this day, signed, sealed, published and declared by the said
    testator to be his last Will and Testament in our presents, and in the presents of each other
    who at his request and in his presents did in the presents of each other have subscribed our
    names thereto as witnesses this 9. Sept. 1862, Edward Bevins of Wilton, Saratoga County,
    Edmund R. Woolley of Wilton, Saratoga County, N. Y."
    I, Cornelius A. Waldron, Surrogate of the County of Saratoga do hereby certify the foregoing
    to be a true record of the last Will and Testament of Jason Adams deceased, relating to Real
    and personal estate, and of the proof thereof taken before me on the 14th day of February
    1863. C. A. Waldron Surrogate.
  Notes:     Proves Lewis M. Adams's father: Jason names "my three sons Leonard Adams, Lewis M.
             Adams and Charles Adams" and daughters Laura Gilbert, Harriet Woolley (wife of Nelson)
             and Orrinda Woolley (wife of Harry [?]), and his wife "Mary or Polly as she is
             commonally coled." Jason died on or about 9 Jan 1863; the will is dated 9 Sept 1862
             and was proved 14 Feb 1863. Lewis had lived on and farmed the "forty two acre lot"
             north of the road, rented from Visscher. "J. R. Adams son of Charles Adams" is a
             grandson. The task's Index to Wills route (DGS 005115924, drawer 055 folder 60) was
             not needed: Full-Text search reached the will book directly. The original papers in

    [p. 309] In the Name of God Amen. I William Woodberry Sen. of Beverly in the County of Essex
    in the Province of the Massachusetts Bay in New England Mariner being through Mercy of
    Disposeing Mind & Memory although very Infirme of Body thro Weakness that hath long attended
    Me therefore Mindfull of My Near Approching Change I Doe Make this My Last Will & Testament
    in Manner following & first I bequeath & Commend My Soul to God who gave [it] hopeing that
    whenever he Shall Call Me hence by Death I Shall have through the Meritts of Jesus Christ an
    Enterence into Eternall Life and when My Body Do returne to Dust from whence itt was taken,
    Shall be raised up in Glory att the Last Day, and I Desire that itt May when I Dye have a
    Deacent Interment att the Discretion of My Executors hereafter Named. And as to the
    Temporall Estate, that God hath bestowed upon Me My Will is.
    Imprimis That all My Just Debts and Funerall Charges be first Paid and Discharged.
    Item I Will and bequeath to My Loving Wife Joanna, My Now Dwelling House and all My Household
    Stuff as beds and beding Bedsteds and all other mooveables in the House and all the Corn and
    Meat and other Provision that is in the House, or Shall be Brought in for y Winter Store and
    all Wooll & Cloth that is in the House and Flaxe that is in the Barn and all her Wareing
    Apparell and the Creatures hereafter Named (viz) Two Cows, and Six good Sheep all which Shee
    Shall have Libberty to Chuse out of My Stock, And also My Horse & halfe the Corn and halfe the
    Apples and halfe the Cyder, that Shall be raised on My Living, with halfe the Pastureing and
    Carbidge [?] of the Same and halfe the Barn Standing to the Eastward of my house, All Corn &
    Cyder Apples And the halfe of Everything that is Raised on My Land before given to My Wife
    Joannah, My five Sons Shall bring into the House for her & halfe the Hay Stalks and Straw and
    Winter Meet [meat] for the Creatures, halfe of all Sorts, they My five Sons Shall put itt into
    the Barn yearly for her, Also My Garden before the Door of My House and the Use of the Shop,
    And One Swine Now att the Door and also My five Sons Shall Cutt and bring to My Wife five
    Cord of Wood yearly of[f] My Land All before bequeathed to My Loveing Wife Joannah to be for
    her own Use Dureing the time of her Widowhood So long as Shee Shall remain My Widow & No
    longer. And If My Wife Shall See Cause to Change her Condition and Marry Again, then I give
    and bequeath to My Loveing Wife Joannah forever to be att her own Disposall the Bed she Lyes
    on Standing in the West End of My House, with all the furniture thereunto belonging, And One
    Iron Pott, and One Brass Kittle, fire Shovell and Tongs One Trammell, One Pine Chest, & One
    Square Table Standing in the East Room and three Puter Platters, Six Chairs & Six Spoons &
    also Two Cows to be att her own Dispose forever.
    [p. 310] Item My Will is and I hereby give and bequeath to My two Daughters (viz.) Sarah the
    Wife of Ralph Ellenwood Jun[r] And Loes Woodberry to Each of them and their Heires the Sum of
    Ten Pounds apeace to Each of My Said Loveing Daughters in Province Bills of Creditt, or Mony
    Equovalent thereunto, to be paid in Eighteen Months Next After My Decease. Item I give to My
