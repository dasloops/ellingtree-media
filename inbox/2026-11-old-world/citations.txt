KEY POINTS
- Louis (Ludwig Wilhelm) Borell was born at Waldrohrbach on 20 Feb 1844 and baptized the
  next day, son of Jacob Borell, schoolteacher, and Elisabetha Bauer (Waldhambach Catholic
  parish books, Waldrohrbach section). The parish family book lists the whole family:
  Jacob (born Hainfeld, son of Heinrich Borell and Francisca Hauck, died at Eschbach 1886),
  his two wives, and twelve children, four of them marked "Amerika": Georg Joseph (1834),
  Ludwig Wilhelm (1844), Maria Margaretha (1846) and Christina Ludovica (1848). Baptisms
  of all four are here, with the 1857 marriage of their brother Georg Jacob.
- Adam Haberkorn ("Havercorn"), 50, farmer, his wife Eve and son George, 20, were in
  Dubuque County, Iowa, in 1850. Alois Lang was not found.
- The Kuchenmeisters arrived at New York on 2 Jul 1859 in the bark Union, Captain J.
  Horstmann, from Bremen, with Andreas Oehrlein's family; the list adds an infant,
  Christine, 11 months, and records that Adam Oehrlein, 3 months, died at sea.
- The Haberkorns' 1844 arrival was not found.
- Nova Scotia land petition index cards: Malcolm McMillan of Lochaber settled about 1808-9
  on land beside his father's, had 5 sons and 1 daughter in 1815; a Hugh McMillan born on
  the Ardgour estate, Argyll, came in 1806 and served in the 79th; Donald Campbell and
  Alpin McDougal shared a grant in Pictou District in 1831.
- Henry Stultz, blacksmith, born New Jersey about 1807, is found in every census asked
  for: Beaver Dam 1850, 1860 and 1870, Hastings 1880, with his wife Sally and their
  children. His 1840 entry was not identified.
- Peter Teisen ("Tyson") and Dorothea Kuchenmeister ("Dorrita Coochinmaster") married in
  Carver County on 1 Jan 1864.
