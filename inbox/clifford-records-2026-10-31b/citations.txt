KEY POINTS
- A Moses Clifford, born 3 May 1770 at Rumney, son of Isaac and Ruth (item 11), was in
  Wentworth, Grafton County, NH, in 1800 beside Isaac, Isaac Jr, Reuben and Timothy Clifford
  (item 3). A Moses Clifford of Wentworth, wife Temperance, had a son Peter born in Wentworth
  about 20 Jan 1808 (item 8). A Moses Clifford of Washington, Vermont, had a daughter Laura
  born 19 Apr 1808 (batch 1, item 3b). Births three months apart in two states: the Wentworth
  Moses and the Washington Moses are very unlikely to be one man. Ancestry's tree gives
  Orlando's father the Wentworth Moses's birth date.
- A Moses Clifford of Londonderry, NH, and wife Esther had a son Philander, born Aug 1813
  (item 7). A compiled family sheet with no sources shown names Orlando's parents as "Moses &
  Esther Hinds" (item 18). Nothing found connects the Londonderry Moses to the Washington,
  Vermont, Moses.
- An Israel Clifford is documented in Rockingham County, NH: private in a company that marched
  from Chester in 1777 (item 4); of Deerfield, petitioning in 1779 about his minor son Tristram
  (item 1); a household head in Epsom in 1790 (item 2). None of these names a son Moses.
- The Knox Family (1905): Delia Knox, daughter of John Knox and Bettie McDaniel, married
  Orlando H. Clifford; children Maria, Henry, Mortimer and John (item 6). This names Delia as
  John's mother. Maria is not in any census found so far.
- Orlando H. Clifford "of Indiana" received a federal land patent of 40.49 acres in the
  Lexington, Missouri, land district on 1 May 1843 (item 5).
- Nebraska City newspapers: an eating house at Eastport, Iowa, 1868 (item 12); "the Clifford
  House" burned, April 1871 (item 13); a new cider mill at East Nebraska City, Dec 1871
  (item 14); "O. H. Clifford, deceased", 1 Feb 1873 (item 15). None of the contemporary items
  found mentions ice; the ice business appears only in a later typescript and the compiled
  sheet (items 17, 18).
- Somerset Lodge No. 206, Missouri, 1865: Eli Bruner, Senior Warden; H. Bruner, member
  (item 9). Nothing on the page connects them to Emma Bruner.

petition-1779-deerfield-clifford.jpg              [item 1]
  Source:    supplied by the owner (Ancestry); collection not recorded. Archive page number
             256 written top left and right; "21 Dec 1779" written at top.
  Held by:   [not shown; New Hampshire legislative petitions, presumably the NH State Archives]
  Transcription:
    To the honble the Council and house of Representatives of the State of New Hampshire
    In General Assembly convened
    Humbly Shews
    Israel Clifford of Deerfield in the County of Rockingham Yeoman: That in the year 1777
    his Son Tristram Clifford then a Minor under twenty one Years of age engaged under Capt.
    Nathan Sanborn in the Expedition against Genl. Burgoyne at Saratoga, that the Day of the
    Convention took place he was taken sick and so continued there till some time in november
    when Your Petitioner at very great Expence was obliged to make a journey thither in order
    to help his said son home - that after his return he continued infirm & unwell the whole
    of the following Winter whereby the most of his time was lost & yr Poor Petitioner was at
    the Expence of his Maintenance - Wherefore he prays yr honors to take the Matter into
    Consideration & grant him such allowance as you shall deem reasonable & as bound shall
    pray &c
    Decr 21st 1779                                        Israel Clifford
  Notes:     "The Convention" is the Saratoga Convention, Burgoyne's surrender, 17 Oct 1777.
             Tristram was under 21 in 1777, so born after about 1756. The typescript in item 10
             gives Israel (b. 1728, son of Isaac) a brother Tristram b. 1738 who died unmarried;
             this Tristram is a different, younger man. Nothing here says which Israel this is.

13. The Fairbury Gazette (Fairbury, Nebraska), Sat 22 Apr 1871, p. 2, reprinting the Neb. City
    Chronicle. Newspapers.com, https://www.newspapers.com/image-view/672516848/:
    "THE CLIFFORD HOUSE BURNED.-About nine o'clock on Wednesday night, the 'Clifford House'
    took fire and in a few minutes was a mass of ruins. The house was situated near the levee
    and one of the old landmarks of the city.-The family of Mr. Clifford occupied the building
    and was so taken by surprise that their entire furniture was destroyed. The building was of
    wood and very dry, and burned like a pile of shavings. The Phoenix Insurance Company of New
    York had a risk of one thousand dollars on the house, but no insurance on the furniture.
    The fire occurred through a defective flue."

17. Typescript, "Orlando Hines Clifford", page 1 (Ancestry user upload; author not named,
    written by a grandchild of Mortimer Harrison Clifford; date unknown). Not uploaded:
    unpublished. Its claims, in brief: Mortimer came to Nebraska City in 1855 with his parents,
    "Orlando Hines Clifford and mother Elizabeth Jane (Knox) Clifford", and an older and a
    younger brother; Orlando b. 1812, Vermont; Elizabeth Jane Knox b. 1823, Kentucky; Henry Clay
    ("Uncle Hank") b. 1839, Indiana; Mortimer Harrison b. 1842 and John Marshall b. 1845, both
    thought born in Missouri; O. H. "listed as a carpenter in all the early census records";
    in the 1860s "the first man in Nebraska City to make a business of selling ice"; in 1864
    "put up 400 pounds of ice"; called "the oldest ice dealer in the city"; ran for city
    council in 1859 and 1864 and was defeated; opened the Clifford House in April 1860; in
    June 1860 "employed two domestics, two men, and a boy".
    Checked against records: the wife named here merges Delila/Delia Knox (m. 1835, batch 1
    item 4; item 6) and Elizabeth J. Hunter, born Kentucky (m. 1854, batch 1 item 5). The 1850,
    1860 and 1870 censuses give Trader, Hotel Keeper and Huckster, not carpenter. Henry was 15
    in 1850 (born about 1835), not born 1839. The June 1860 staff matches the 1860 census
    (batch 1, item 2) line for line, so the writer probably used that census. Sources for the
    ice business and the council elections are not given.
