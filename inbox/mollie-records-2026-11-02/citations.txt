             https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-C34V-396T-N  (last page)
             image 643 of 701, DGS 008620209 ("Pioneer, Pierce, Washington ... Genealogies
             1970", also DAR (Washington), v. 24-25)
  Heading:   "MRS. HARRIET LOW HOLBROOK / A TRIBUTE BY HER DAUGHTER / COMMEMORATING HER
             COMING TO WASHINGTON TERRITORY, 1855"  pp. 308-310
  Signed:    (Signed) Mrs. Frances Holbrook Pfeiffer. Contributed by Herbert F. Rowland.
  Not uploaded: a typescript compiled by the DAR, neither a government record nor a
             publication before 1931. Its date is not on these pages (it mentions "the
             World War", so after 1918). For the owner to decide.
  What it says (my summary of the points of fact, in order):
    - Harriet's father's line from Thomas Low, who came with his family to Essex, Mass.,
      in Nov 1636 on a near-disastrous voyage; the Lows from Boxford and Polstead, England.
    - She lists famous kin, among them Ralph Waldo Emerson and "her daughter's great and
      beloved teacher, Dr. Charles Wesley Emerson" [the founder of Emerson College].
    - Harriet's maternal great-grandfather a Revolutionary colonel made brigadier general
      and a Massachusetts judge [Gen. Ebenezer Tyler [?], not named here]; her grandfather
      Rowland Tyler of Dixmont [spelled "Dixmount"], Maine, justice and teacher, War of
      1812; her father Nathan Low, son of Major Nathan Low, born on Deer Isle, a seaman,
      drowned 1874 when his vessel was sunk by a Portland steamboat.
    - Harriet was the eldest of ten; her mother, the eldest daughter of Rowland Tyler, was
      a teacher, and often ill.
    - Married at sixteen to a widower, Mr. Sylvester (brother of Edmund Sylvester, the
      founder of Olympia); came out in 1855 by Panama; storm-bound a week at Capt. Coupe's
      house at Coupeville; the steamer Water-Lily sank on the mud flats on her way to
      Olympia. The marriage was annulled by the legislature; she kept her maiden name and
      sewed for a living.
    - Capt. Coupe introduced her to Capt. Holbrook in Olympia (Holbrook was there as a
      delegate to the Territorial Legislature); married at Steilacoom, at the home of Mr.
      and Mrs. Ford, Christmas Eve 1860; came up Penn Cove by canoe and landed in front of
      Mrs. Libby's home.
    - She ploughed the first spring at San de Fuca; midwife and nurse to neighbours
      ("Aunty Holbrook"); taught Indian women knitting, sewing and cooking.
    - Died aged 84 at her son Richard Holbrook's, after twelve years crippled by a broken
      hip; won prizes and a medal for knitted lace.
    - Frances writes of herself as "a certain girl-child" spanked with the slipper.
    - It closes with her own reflections on pioneer stock, and on "the distaff side" being
      left out of the old records.
  Notes:     Her authorship is stated by the signature, and the content (Holbrook, Low, San
             de Fuca, Emerson) fits her. The task says Harriet died at her son's house at 560
             Mercer St., Seattle; this agrees that it was Richard's. The 1860 marriage date
             and the earlier Sylvester marriage may be new to the archive. Who Herbert F.
             Rowland was is not stated.

  i135, read from the page images:
    Letterhead: THE CINEMA LYCEUM, 714-715 Story Building, Los Angeles. In the top right
    corner two names: PHILIP A. THOMPSON / FRANCES H. PFEIFFER. Dated 6/16/16. To Dr. W. E.
    Burghardt Du Bois, Editor The Crisis, 70 Fifth Ave., New York.
    In summary: his book The Negro interests her keenly; she writes partly because she has
    heard her husband say "he was with you in Harvard, and belonged, I believe, to the same
    fraternity"; early that spring she had the idea of "a great Negro picture" as the
    Lyceum's first production, based on Booker T. Washington's life, now grown into a
    larger plan; the Lyceum's purpose is educational pictures; they will not begin
    financing until they have the approval of prominent men and women of the race; she
    has had no answer yet from Mr. Scott and asks Du Bois to suggest people in Los Angeles
    to work with. Closing: "If Mr. Pfeiffer were here I am sure he would wish to be kindly
    remembered to you." Signed in ink: Frances H. Pfeiffer. [Archivist's date pencilled
    in the margin: 2/9/17 [?]]
  Notes:     Credo's own description calls Philip A. Thompson her husband; the letter does
             not. Thompson is only the second name on the letterhead, and the husband who
             was at Harvard is "Mr. Pfeiffer", absent from Los Angeles in June 1916. The
             Story Building address and Thompson as a partner may be new to the archive.
